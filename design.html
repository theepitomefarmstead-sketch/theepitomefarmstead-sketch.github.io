Regenerative land planning

Design with the land.

Thoughtful farm plans begin by reading the place: its water, terrain, soil, ecology, and the people who care for it.

A whole-site perspective

A connected plan for a living landscape.

Regenerative land design brings food production and ecological care into one site-specific plan. Rather than treating fields, water, trees, and buildings as separate pieces, it considers how each can support the others over time.

Planning areas

Design that connects the pieces.

Depending on the site and goals, a design can explore these connected elements as part of a phased plan.

01

Whole-site layout

Map zones, access, growing areas, and built spaces around the land's conditions and intended use.

02

Water & terrain

Consider slope, runoff, storage, and water movement when planning resilient landscapes.

03

Agroforestry & planting

Plan trees, perennial crops, windbreaks, and planting patterns suited to the local context.

04

Soil & fertility

Identify opportunities to protect soil, build organic matter, and cycle nutrients within the farm.

05

Integrated food systems

Connect gardens, crops, livestock, compost, and local food pathways where appropriate.

06

Practical phasing

Organize ideas into manageable steps that can be adapted as the land and project change.

From plan to practice

Support can continue beyond the drawing.

Implementation needs depend on the land, available resources, and the people involved. Possible next steps can be scoped to fit the project.

Site assessment

Walk the land and record conditions, opportunities, and questions to guide planning.

Implementation guidance

Break selected design priorities into clear, practical steps.

Knowledge sharing

Help farmers and project teams understand the reasoning behind design choices.

Follow-up learning

Review observations and update plans as the site responds and conditions evolve.

Who this is for

For people caring for land and livelihoods.

Land planning is most useful when it reflects the needs of the people who will steward, work, and learn from the landscape.

  • Farmers & growers
  • Landowners
  • Community groups
  • Cooperatives
  • Learning projects
  • Local organizations
